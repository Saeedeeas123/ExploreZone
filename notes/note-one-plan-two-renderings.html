One plan, two renderings: why the lips cannot disagree with the sound

Verified end-to-endDSPArchitecture

The usual approach

Talking characters are normally built as two pipelines that are then synchronised: audio is generated on one side, mouth shapes on the other, and a mapping layer tries to keep them together. Every synchronisation bug in that design is a bug about agreement between two independent computations.

The alternative

Generate an intermediate plan first — a timed sequence of speech units with rhythm and pitch — and then render it twice: once as audio, once as control curves for the face. The mouth and the sound are two renderings of the same object, so they cannot disagree. There is no synchronisation layer, because there is nothing to synchronise. 

text → phonemes → plan (timing, pitch, articulation)
                    ├── render A → audio
                    └── render B → jaw / width / rounding curves → face

What it produced

The full loop runs end to end: a word spoken in voices the system was never trained on is recognised, answered, spoken back over four and a half seconds, and rendered as 38 animated frames of a face with blinks, gaze movement and brow accents — delivering a WAV file and an animated image.

Some of the measurements that came out of the same pipeline:

  • Articulation is geometrically real: closed mouth 2.4 mm, open vowel 15.3 mm, a mid vowel 6.3 mm, and a plosive seals to 1.7 mm.
  • Mirror symmetry is exact — 0.000000 mm deviation across 2,511 sampled points.
  • The synthesised voice's formants are recoverable to within about 11.5% by an independent analysis, which is how the voice was checked rather than assumed.

Why this is the interesting part

The design does not fix synchronisation bugs; it removes the class. That is the better move whenever it is available: make the wrong state unrepresentable instead of detecting it.

The same reasoning shows up elsewhere in my work — a task model where termination is a property of the structure rather than a rule to remember, and a validation gate that checks a translation against its source rather than trusting the translator.

Honest limits Measured-correct is not the same as convincing. The face is low-poly: proportions and articulation are in human ranges and were measured, but visual realism was rated roughly 1–2 out of 10 by the person who tested it — no eyelids, angular ears, no textures. The architecture is sound; the renderer is the gap.
