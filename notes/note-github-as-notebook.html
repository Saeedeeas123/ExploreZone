GitHub as a lab notebook, honestly assessed

Sources checkedOpen scienceMethod

Why this entry exists

This site is the answer to a question I asked myself: can a public repository serve as a working notebook on the way to something worth citing — and does opening it create leverage or just exposure? The short answer is yes to the first, and “only in specific ways” to the second.

It is an established practice, not an invention

The name is Open Notebook Science: publishing the researcher's complete record rather than only the finished result. The term comes from Jean-Claude Bradley, who framed it as full transparency — exposing the complete record of the work as it happens. Carl Boettiger, an ecologist, has run a fully open, version-controlled lab notebook since 2010 and describes his repositories as an inverted form of the notebook: grouped by project rather than by chronology.

What a repository genuinely provides

PropertyMechanismWhy it matters
Timestamped provenanceEvery entry is a commit with a hash and a timeA chronological record of when an idea existed — the digital equivalent of a dated notebook page
Preserved reasoningHistory, branches, issuesThe evolution of thinking survives, not just the final state
CitabilityTagged releases archived by a data repository, each with a permanent DOIThis is the step that turns a repository into something a paper can cite
A public nodeStatic site hosting from the same repositoryA permanent, free address for the work — which is what this site is

The citability mechanism is the important one, and it is official and documented rather than folklore:

  • GitHub Docs — referencing and citing content
  • Zenodo — enabling a repository for DOI issuance
  • CodeRefinery — making a project citable

Practitioners' notebooks, for reference: cboettig/labnotebook and his write-up on running it.

What it does not provide

  • It is not a priority instrument. A commit timestamp is strong informal evidence of when something existed, but it is not a legal record — and public disclosure before filing destroys patent novelty in most jurisdictions. If something is patentable, publishing is a decision, not a default.
  • It is not a citation by itself. Without the release-to-DOI step, a repository is a URL, not a reference.
  • It does not create an audience. The measured take-up of open notebooks is very low — one study put it around 5% — and the literature documents a clear reluctance to expose work immediately. Nobody arrives because the notebook is open; they arrive because a paper, a post or a project points at it.
  • It does not protect you from being scooped. Public is discoverable. The mitigation is about what you publish, not how you publish it.

The rule I adopted

Publish directions, methods, failures and findings. Hold concrete competitive results until they are citable. One-way flow: private notes become public entries after review — never a dump.

Which is why this notebook contains root causes, dead ends and design reasoning, and no unreleased product detail. The filter is deliberate, and it is also why the entries are useful to a reader: a filtered notebook is a knowledge artifact, an unfiltered one is a diary.

Confidence The citability mechanism and the hosting facts are verified from primary documentation. The practice (named researchers running public notebooks) is documented. The adoption figures come from published studies and are reported as such — they are the weakest link in this entry and the one most likely to be dated.
