The voice-as-touch category is structurally empty

Research completeLandscapeAccessibility

The question

Not “can a voice assistant do things on a phone” — that is solved many times over. The question was narrower: does a voice replacement for touch primitives exist? Seven commands only — open, close, back, forward, next page, scroll up, scroll down — with near-zero delay, the user's own wording, and improvement over iterations. Not an agent that executes whole goals.

Method

  • Three keyword waves across the repository ecosystem, roughly 2,000 repositories surfaced.
  • A code-level search for projects that literally combine speech recognition with global accessibility actions — the signature of this exact category.
  • 42 projects curated with API-verified metadata (stars, forks, last activity, licence).
  • 12 issue trackers mined for user-reported failure evidence, not marketing claims.

Findings

The ecosystem converged on the model that was explicitly not wanted. The visible, popular projects are one-shot agents that interpret a sentence and drive the phone — several with hundreds of thousands of stars. The primitive layer — voice as a faster touch — exists only as single-author repositories with a handful of stars and no releases.

Feature-level, it exists. A first-party accessibility service already ships the exact command list, including auto-resuming listening. But it is closed source, offers no latency control, no personal vocabulary, and no choice of offline model. Feature-level: exists. Requirement-level: does not. That gap is the project.

Stars mislead. One of the most visible projects in the space does not build at its current HEAD — hundreds of compile errors. Two others are licensed for personal use only, so they cannot be reused in a product. Popularity is not evidence of usability.

Failure modes worth designing around

Mined from real issue trackers rather than guessed:

FailureEvidence sourceDesign response
Always-on microphone blocks other apps and drains batteryTwo independent voice projectsOwn the microphone through a foreground service of the correct type; handle boot start; expect battery-whitelisting on aggressive ROMs
Service starts blocked by the OSCommunity voice-assistant projectAssume the OS will kill it; design recovery, not just startup
Wake word fails while media is playingOpen voice-assistant projectNever let detection be the only gate — keep a manual trigger
Non-English recognition quality is weakSpeech-model demo issuesKeep command words in English (the device locale is already English)
Memory grows without bound during repeated inferenceSpeech-engine issue report (RAM to 2 GB)Test a multi-hour always-on run before trusting it

Decision that came out of it

Two paths were compared: keyword spotting (tiny models, phrase list editable in a text file with no retraining, millisecond detection) versus streaming speech recognition with an intent matcher (open phrasing, but hundreds of milliseconds of latency and a much larger model). The first was chosen because it is the only one that feels like touch; the second was deferred, not rejected.

And the most useful conclusion was architectural: this does not need to be a new app. The action layer — global actions, gesture dispatch, overlay, foreground service — already existed and was proven across nine builds of an earlier project. The work is one new input source in front of a proven pipeline.

Scope of the claim Star counts, licence status and repository activity were captured during the sweep and are not re-verified on this page today — the finding that matters is structural: the primitive layer is absent and the agent layer is crowded. Re-check any specific repository before relying on it.
