Why the numbers do not matter here

This entire site — every page, every stylesheet — is a few tens of kilobytes. The 1 GB site limit is roughly four orders of magnitude away. At a typical page weight, 100 GB per month of bandwidth is on the order of a hundred thousand page views. And the ten-builds-per-hour limit only applies to the built-in build path; a custom workflow removes it entirely.

The practical rule that comes out of this: do not host large media in the repository. Keep images small and keep video elsewhere. That is the only way a normal text site gets anywhere near the limits.

The three limits that actually constrain

  1. Static only. No server-side code, no database, no custom headers or server-side redirects, no secret storage. Anything dynamic has to be client-side or an external service.
  2. No commercial use. The documentation is explicit that this is not a free host for an online business, an e-commerce site, or software-as-a-service, and that it should not be used for sensitive transactions such as passwords or card numbers. A personal CV, portfolio, blog, project documentation or academic notebook is fine. A product landing page that sells is not.
  3. Free plan means public repositories. Since a CV or notebook is meant to be public, this is usually a non-issue — but it rules out a private staging site without a paid plan.

Also worth knowing: visitor IP addresses are logged for security purposes regardless of sign-in.

So what are those blog-like sites?

They are the same service with a static site generator on top. Two URL shapes exist: a user site at <user>.github.io and a project site at <user>.github.io/<repository>. The generators in common use are Jekyll (built natively), Hugo, Astro, and — for anyone with an Obsidian vault — Quartz, which is designed to publish a vault as a site with wikilinks, backlinks and search.

The architectural conclusion

Static hosting is the right home for the identity-and-proof node — the CV, the notebook, the project documentation. It is the wrong home for anything that transacts. Those are two different sites, and they should not be forced into one.
