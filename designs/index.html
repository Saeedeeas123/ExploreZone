Design lab — 10 graphic directions

Ten completely different visual treatments. Every page carries exactly the same content — same headline, same three projects, same numbers, same notebook teaser. So any difference you feel is design, not content.

01 Swiss / International Typographic → Strict grid, hairline rules, huge Helvetica, one red accent. Nothing decorative — pure information hierarchy. Reads as serious and institutional. 02 Terminal / CRT → Phosphor green on black, scanlines, ASCII boxes, blinking cursor, real shell prompts. Signals “engineer” instantly — and unapologetically. 03 Editorial / Magazine → Serif type, masthead, drop cap, two-column text, pull quote, cream paper. Treats the work as writing to be read — best if the notebook is the main product. 04 Neubrutalism → Thick black borders, hard offset shadows, loud flat colour, tilted section labels. Confident and memorable; risks reading as playful over serious. 05 Glass / Aurora → Frosted translucent cards floating over blurred colour blobs, gradient text, soft depth. Modern product-site feel; heavier to render on old phones. 06 Bento Grid → Modular tiles of unequal size, big radii, soft shadows, numbers as hero tiles. Very legible on a phone and easy to extend with new blocks later. 07 Blueprint / Technical Drawing → Gridded drafting paper, cyan linework, dashed part outlines, dimension rules, a real title block with sheet and revision. Makes engineering the brand. 08 Paper Lab Notebook → Ruled paper, red margin, taped corners, index cards, a rotated “verified” stamp. The most literal reading of “open lab notebook”. 09 Cyberpunk HUD → Neon magenta and cyan on near-black, clipped corners, glitch-shadowed headings, telemetry readouts. Maximum energy; the loudest of the ten. 10 Minimal Luxury → Vast whitespace, tiny letterspaced capitals, hairline rules, a single gold accent, serif statement type. The most “expensive” and the most restrained.

How to judge: open each one and look at three things only — the headline, the project block, and the numbers row. Then ask: does this feel like the person I want to be seen as, and can I read it on a phone in sunlight? Mixed answers are allowed — a direction can be taken from one and details from another.
