Design lab — 10 graphic directions

Ten completely different visual treatments. Every page carries exactly the same content — same headline, same three projects, same numbers, same notebook teaser. So any difference you feel is design, not content.

Round 1 — ten directions

01 Swiss / International Typographic → Strict grid, hairline rules, huge Helvetica, one red accent. Nothing decorative — pure information hierarchy. Reads as serious and institutional. 02 Terminal / CRT → Phosphor green on black, scanlines, ASCII boxes, blinking cursor, real shell prompts. Signals “engineer” instantly — and unapologetically. 03 Editorial / Magazine → Serif type, masthead, drop cap, two-column text, pull quote, cream paper. Treats the work as writing to be read — best if the notebook is the main product. 04 Neubrutalism →your pick Thick black borders, hard offset shadows, loud flat colour, tilted section labels. Confident and memorable; risks reading as playful over serious. 05 Glass / Aurora → Frosted translucent cards floating over blurred colour blobs, gradient text, soft depth. Modern product-site feel; heavier to render on old phones. 06 Bento Grid → Modular tiles of unequal size, big radii, soft shadows, numbers as hero tiles. Very legible on a phone and easy to extend with new blocks later. 07 Blueprint / Technical Drawing → Gridded drafting paper, cyan linework, dashed part outlines, dimension rules, a real title block with sheet and revision. Makes engineering the brand. 08 Paper Lab Notebook → Ruled paper, red margin, taped corners, index cards, a rotated “verified” stamp. The most literal reading of “open lab notebook”. 09 Cyberpunk HUD → Neon magenta and cyan on near-black, clipped corners, glitch-shadowed headings, telemetry readouts. Maximum energy; the loudest of the ten. 10 Minimal Luxury → Vast whitespace, tiny letterspaced capitals, hairline rules, a single gold accent, serif statement type. The most “expensive” and the most restrained.

Round 2 — ten more, tuned to your taste · same content, different structural idea each

11 Poster / Type-Driven → Typography is the layout. Massive uppercase headline, outlined numerals you can see through, one electric blue accent, no boxes or borders at all — only rules and scale. 12 Riso Print / Overprint → Two spot inks on cream paper, a halftone dot field, and deliberate misregistration — every heading casts a second-ink shadow, as if the plates slipped. Screen-print energy. 13 Constructivist → A diagonal red band cuts the page. Heavy black bars, an arrow, a rotated tagline, numerals in red — political-poster geometry. Loud and disciplined at once. 14 Sticker Bomb / Zine → Every block is a die-cut sticker: white edge, black outline, its own tilt, one acid colour. Tape, dots and a marker feel — chaotic on top of a strict grid underneath. 15 Arcade / 8-bit → Chunky bevelled panels with inset light and shade, a dotted pixel grid, a credits HUD, and metrics presented as high scores. Playful, but every edge is deliberate. 16 Data Poster / Telemetry → A black masthead, a strip of spec plates, tabular rows, and numbers at poster scale with tabular figures. Dense and factual — the design of a spec sheet, not a brochure. 17 Memphis / 80s Postmodern → Primary colours, outline circles, triangles and pills floating in the margin, rounded chunky cards each with a different coloured shadow. The most fun of the twenty. 18 Xerox / Photocopy → Greyscale only, a dithered noise field, a dashed inner frame, a rotated “verified original” stamp and inverted black blocks. Raw, cheap-to-print, deliberately unpolished. 19 Industrial / Hazard → Safety yellow against black hazard stripes, a caution plate, rivets on the cards, steel spec plates and metrics as gauge readings. Reads as heavy equipment, not software. 20 Grid-Break Editorial Poster → A colour block sits behind the headline and the headline runs straight over it, breaking the grid on purpose. Outlined numerals, a vertical side label, hairline rules.

How to judge: open each one and look at three things only — the headline, the project block, and the numbers row. Then ask: does this feel like the person I want to be seen as, and can I read it on a phone in sunlight? Mixed answers are allowed — a direction can be taken from one and details from another.
